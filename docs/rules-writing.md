# Rules for writing The Narrow Path

Distilled from the author's change requests. A reviewer checks the page against these; a writer applies them. The rules for research are in `rules-research.md`.

## Words

1. One word, one meaning; one meaning, one word. (Never "type"; always "type expression" or "type set". Never "terminates"; "strongly normalizing" or `∈ SN`.)
2. Standard mathematical names, each explained plainly once before use.
3. Names, not pointers: no "the first two", "above", "below", or a bare label such as "W2" without its name.
4. Conditions, requirements and the parts of a lemma get meaningful names, not letters.

## Statements

5. "If … then …", never "whenever" or "X when Y".
6. Every quantifier and every hypothesis is explicit, including why a seemingly missing hypothesis is not needed.
7. One definition per sentence and per line; never tucked inside a lemma, an example or another definition.
8. A recurring collection or operation is defined once, with notation, before use, and then used.
9. Concrete before general.
10. Contrasts are stated outright ("over type sets, NOT over type expressions").

## Logic

11. The logical status and scope of every claim is explicit: definition, modeling choice, theorem proved here, quoted, worked out here and not reviewed.
12. Top-level logic is explicit; no skipped step; a failure is explained step by step.
13. Nothing is used before it is defined; a fact sits where it is used.
14. Structural observations are wanted, as lemmas with examples.

## Structure

15. Definition, then examples, then narrow points; simple examples first; special cases that do not earn their place go.
16. Each example in its own frame; the main line never depends on an example frame.
17. Colour marks four things only (definition, lemma or theorem, example, narrow point); proofs are folded and uncoloured; the page does not explain its own apparatus and does not over-advertise narrow points.
18. Anything said to the author in chat about the status of the page belongs in the page.
19. Sections are small, in dependency order; every section lists what it uses; every earlier section a text refers to is among the sections it uses, apart from passing mentions.
