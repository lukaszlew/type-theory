### A1. "do something that other type sets cannot" is false by section 39 itself.

Quoted, line 3083: "§39 is where sets given by tests do something that other type sets cannot: for the terms of Parigot's λμ-calculus a name stands for a test, the rule that makes a μ-abstraction is false over all type sets, and it is true over the sets defined by tests."

### A3. Under "Quoted." the note states an inference that is not in Riba's paper.

### A5. "The item exactly says for which s and U it holds": the item proves "if" for the fact about unions, and "only if" for a wider statement.

Quoted, line 3033: "The item exactly says for which s and U it holds; how often the member is used does not decide it." ("it" is the fact of section 22 over TT, "with the smallest test-defined set above the union in the place of the union and with U test-defined".) The same claim is in the example, line 3013: "says for which uses of a member the fact of §22 holds with (⋃C F(C))° in the place of the union."

Why. The item is about the statement "for every nonempty set S of strongly normalizing terms". The fact of section 22 over TT is about the sets S = ⋃C F(C) only, which are unions of nonempty collections of test-defined sets (every nonempty collection of members of TT is the set of values of some F). "If W is empty or test-defined, the fact holds" passes to the narrower statement. "Only if" does not: its proof takes S = W, and W need not be a union of test-defined sets. So for the fact about existential types the page has a sufficient condition, and the two counterexamples (unchanged, one use) for two particular s.

What is exact for unions (derived here by hand, three lines, read by no one else). Write W′ for the union of the test-defined sets that are inside W. The fact holds for s and U, for every F from TT to TT, if and only if W′ is empty or test-defined. If W′ is empty no union of members of TT is inside W. If W′ is test-defined and ⋃C F(C) ⊆ W, then each F(C) is inside W′, so (⋃C F(C))° ⊆ W′ ⊆ W. In the other direction take the collection of all test-defined sets inside W: its union is W′, so W′° ⊆ W; W′° is test-defined, so it is inside W′; so W′ = W′°. I found no s and U for which this and "W is empty or test-defined" differ, and I did not prove that there is none.

Fix, minimal: "The item exactly says for which s and U it holds if any nonempty set of strongly normalizing terms may stand in the place of the union: exactly if W is empty or test-defined. For a union of members of TT that condition is enough; that it is needed is proved for arbitrary S only. How often the member is used does not decide it." And in the example: "gives a condition under which the fact of §22 holds with ...".
