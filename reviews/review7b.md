## 1. `good.html`: Lemma "Weak normalization with no theorem quoted"

**Verdict: correct as stated and proved.** Every case is covered, each induction is on the (finite) derivation of membership in `WN` and every appeal to "entered before" is to a premise of the last rule, and "a term enters WN by exactly one rule" is true.

Re-derivation.

- Exactly one rule. The conclusion of *variable applied* is a variable applied to arguments, of *abstraction* an abstraction, of *head step* an abstraction applied to at least one argument (only such a term has a head step). The three forms exclude each other, so the last rule is fixed by the form. The premises are fixed too: the arguments of `x u1..un` are determined, the body of an abstraction is determined (up to the name of the bound variable), and a term has at most one head step. So membership inverts: `x u1..un ∈ WN` gives each `ui ∈ WN`, `λz.r ∈ WN` gives `r ∈ WN`, `(λz.r) s u1..un ∈ WN` gives `r[s/z] u1..un ∈ WN`, each by a shorter derivation.
- Normal forms. Three rule cases, all immediate.
- Renaming. `r[x/y]` has the form of `r` (a variable goes to a variable, so the head stays a variable, an abstraction, or an abstraction with arguments). Variable case: premises `ri[x/y]`. Abstraction case: `(λz.r′)[x/y] = λz.r′[x/y]` for `z` different from `x` and `y`. Redex case: `(r′[x/y])[s[x/y]/z] r1[x/y].. = (r′[s/z] r1..rn)[x/y]` for `z` different from `x`, `y` (substitution lemma). All three use the induction hypothesis on a premise only. The statement is needed, and proved, for a renaming that may identify two variables (`x` may already be free in `r`).
- Applied to a variable. `t` is a variable applied to arguments: the premises of `t x ∈ WN` are the `ui` and `x`. `t` has a head redex: the head step of `t x` is the head step of `t` with `x` appended; induction hypothesis on the premise. `t = λz.r`: the premise is `r[x/z]`, and renaming gives `r ∈ WN`. No freshness of `x` is needed anywhere, which matches the statement of the property in the first paragraph of the section.
- *Variable applied* and *safe head step* of `Good` are the rules (the rule is stronger than *safe head step*).

Findings (none is an error).

1. Quoted: "note first that a term enters `WN` by exactly one rule, the one for its form, because the three forms of {{head}} exclude each other." The exclusion of the forms gives one *rule*; what the two inductions use is one *rule instance*, i.e. that the premises are determined as well. That needs one more clause: "and the premises of that rule are determined by the term, because a term has at most one head step". Suggested wording: "a term enters `WN` by exactly one rule, the one for its form, and from premises that the term determines: the three forms of {{head}} exclude each other, and a term has only one head step."
2. Quoted: "If `r` is `λz.r′`, the same with `r′`." and "If `t` is `λz.r`, then the term after the head step of `t x` is `r[x/z]`". Both silently choose the bound variable `z` different from `x` and `y`, and both then use that `λz.r′ ∈ WN` gives `r′ ∈ WN` for that choice of `z`. On a page that identifies terms up to the names of bound variables this is fine; one clause ("with `z` chosen different from `x` and `y`") makes it explicit. In the last case `z = x` would even make the renaming step unnecessary; choosing `z` different from `x` keeps the text uniform.
3. Quoted (note): "The definition of `WN` by these rules is Matthes's, and so is the remark on where standardization is needed". Not checked by this reader (the thesis was not available to me). The page's own generation lemma note credits the three rules for `SN` to van Raamsdonk and Severi; the `WN` variant "head step without the condition on the argument" is, from general knowledge, also in van Raamsdonk and Severi and in Joachimski and Matthes. Keep the attribution only if the thesis page was read.
4. The statement that standardization is needed "for one thing only ... that every term with a normal form is in `WN`" is right: membership in `WN` is exactly "the leftmost run ends" (abstraction: go under the binder; variable applied: arguments left to right; head step: the leftmost redex of a term with a head redex is the head redex).

Simpler. The renaming lemma and *applied to a variable* can be one induction: prove "if `t x ∈ WN` or `t[x/y] ∈ WN` then `t ∈ WN`" is not shorter; the present split is the clear one. One real shortening: in section 45 (`complete.html`) the weakly normalizing case could use this `WN` as `Good` (it is closed under undoing every head step by its own rule, and contains `B`), which removes that proof's dependence on the standardization theorem; see finding 3.4.

## 2. `tests.html`: Lemma "The same with closed terms, and no closed term that merges"

**Verdict: both items are correct as stated and proved.** One number in the note is wrong (finding 2.1).

Re-derivation of *closed witness*. `t′ a → λf. f (a K) (a K′)` and `a K′ → K′ I δ δ → (λy.y) δ δ → δ δ`; `t′ b → λf. f (b K) (b K′)` and `b K → K δ I δ → (λy.δ) I δ → δ δ`. So `t′` is in neither set. Let `v1..vn` be a test that every member of `Ta ∪ Tb` passes.

- `n = 0`: `t′` is a normal form.
- `n ≥ 1`: `p ∈ Ta`, `q ∈ Tb` pass, so `v1 K, v1 K′ ∈ SN` (R1, from `p v1..vn → v1 K v2..vn`).
- `n = 1`: `t′ v1 →h λf. f (v1 K) (v1 K′)`, safe; the body is in `Base`, so the abstraction is in `SN`; R3.
- `n ≥ 2`: `u = λx.λy. y (v1 K) (v1 K′)`; `u a →h λy. y (v1 K) (v1 K′) ∈ SN`, safe because `a` is a normal form; so `u ∈ Ta` and `u v1..vn ∈ SN`; two head steps of it give `v2 (v1 K) (v1 K′) v3..vn ∈ SN` (R1); `t′ v1..vn` reaches the same term by two head steps with arguments `v1`, `v2 ∈ SN`; R3 twice.

The auxiliary `u` is needed: `p` and `q` alone do not suffice for `n ≥ 2`. With `v1 = I`, `v2 = λa.λb. b I δ δ`, both `p v1 v2` and `q v1 v2` are in `SN` and `t′ v1 v2 → v2 K K′ → K′ I δ δ → Ω`. The proof is right to bring in a member of `Ta` that ignores its first argument.

Re-derivation of *no merging*. `f ∉ SN`: `T = U = SN`, `t = s = x`. `f ∈ SN` with normal form `λx1..λxk. xi u1..um`, `k ≥ 1`: for any terms `wj` at the other places, `f w1..wk+1` reaches `wi u1′..um′ δ` by `k` head steps and `δ δ` by `m` more; the other `wj` occur only inside the `uj′`.

- `k = 1` (so `i = 1`): `t = λz1..zm.δ`, `s = δ`, both normal forms, `T = U = SN`, `f t s` reaches `Ω`.
- `k ≥ 2`, `i = 1`: `y` at place 2, `t = w1 = λz⃗.δ ∈ SN = T`, `s = y ∈ V = U`.
- `k ≥ 2`, `i = 2`: `y` at place 1, `t = y ∈ V = T`, `s = w2 = λz⃗.δ ∈ SN = U`.
- `k ≥ 2`, `i ≥ 3`: both places 1 and 2 are free; `y` at one of them, `I` at the other; same as above with `I` for the term in `SN`.
- In each case the test `w3..wk+1` has `k − 1 ≥ 1` terms, all closed normal forms (`I`, `λz⃗.δ`, `δ`; the variable `y` is at place 1 or 2 and so not in the test), so `V` is test-defined; `y ∈ V` because `y w3..wk+1 ∈ Base`; `V ⊆ SN`, so `T ∩ U = V`; and `f t s w3..wk+1 = f w1..wk+1` reaches `Ω`, so `f t s ∉ V`.

## 3. `complete.html`: Lemma "What the complete semantics proves of a typable term"

**Verdict: correct as stated and proved**, all three items; "normal" is right as stated, and "head" is right for `ω → X`.

Re-derivation.

- Good-set: expansion-closed, inside `Good`, contains `B`. `HN` (head normalizing) and the set of terms with a normal form are closed under undoing any step, contain their `B`. Right.
- "If `t x ∈ Good` for a variable `x` not free in `t`, then `t ∈ Good`", head normalizing case. Run from `t x` to a head normal form `h`. No step contracts the whole term: all steps are inside `t`, `h = t′ x` is an application, so `h = y u1..un` with `un = x`, and `t′ = y u1..un−1` is a head normal form. Some step does: `t` reaches `λy.s`, `s[x/y]` reaches `h`, `x` is not free in `λy.s`, so `λy.s = λx.s[x/y]` reaches `λx.h`. Right. (Freshness of `x` is used here and is available where the claim is applied.)
- `T ⇒ U`. Expansion-closed: as in the lemma before. Inside `Good`: a variable fresh for `t` is in `T`. Contains `B`: first case, `B` allows any arguments, so `T` may be the set of all terms; second case, the argument must be weakly normalizing, which is the extra hypothesis `T ⊆ Good`. Right; the two definitions of `B` are exactly what the two inductions need.
- Head. `ρ(X) = HN` is expansion-closed, so the soundness direction applies. Induction: first layer names a Good-set (`X`: `HN`; `S → A`: the set of `S` contains every variable, by the induction hypothesis for the second layer, and the set of `A` is a Good-set); second layer with `n ≥ 1` names an intersection of Good-sets, which contains `B`, so every variable; `n = 0` names all terms. Every variable of `Γ` is in the set of its `S`, including one declared `ω`. So `t` is in the set of `A`, inside `HN`. For `A = ω → X` the set is `{t : t u ∈ HN for every term u}`, a Good-set by the claim with `T` = all terms; a term in it is head normalizing by the fresh-variable argument. Right.
- Normal. `ρ(X)` = terms with a normal form. Induction over `ω`-free expressions of both layers: `X`; `S → A` with `S = A1 ∧ .. ∧ An`, `n ≥ 1`, an intersection of Good-sets, which is a Good-set, so it contains every variable and is inside `Good`; so the arrow is a Good-set. `S` itself with `n ≥ 1` likewise. `ω` inside the derivation is harmless, because soundness is used as a black box for the whole system and only the sets of `Γ` and `S` are inspected. An intersection `S` is covered by the second layer of the induction. A free variable of `t` that `Γ` does not declare stays itself; nothing is asked of it. Right, and it agrees with the characterization quoted from the survey.
- Not strong. `x:ω, y:X ⊢ y : X` (variable, `X ≤ X`); "→ in" with `S = ω`; `Ω : ω` by "∧ in" with `n = 0`; "→ out". Right.

Findings (no error).

1. Quoted: "By induction, a type expression in which `ω` does not occur names a Good-set." The induction runs over both layers and the text names neither; the reader has to see that an `ω`-free `S` is an intersection of `n ≥ 1` Good-sets and that such an intersection is a Good-set (the Head paragraph says it; this one does not). Fix: "By induction, a type expression of either layer in which `ω` does not occur names a Good-set: a variable names `Good`, an intersection of one or more Good-sets is a Good-set, and for `S → A` the claim on `T ⇒ U` applies, because the set of `S` is a Good-set, so it contains every variable and is inside `Good`."
2. Quoted: "If `Γ ⊢ t : S` is derivable, `S` is not `ω`, and `ω` occurs neither in `Γ` nor in `S`". "`S` is not `ω`" is implied by "`ω` does not occur in `S`" if the empty intersection counts as an occurrence; say which is meant, or drop one. Suggested: "... is derivable, and `ω` occurs neither in `Γ` nor in `S` (so `S` has at least one part)".
3. Quoted: "So each variable of `Γ` may be replaced by itself". In the Head paragraph the reason is "contains every variable"; here it is "contains `B`". Also not said in either paragraph: a free variable of `t` that `Γ` does not declare is left as it is, which the definition of a true judgment allows only if "true" is read as "for every replacement of the variables of the context" (the lemma before says so). One clause would close it.
4. Quoted: "For the weakly normalizing terms this is shown in {{good}}." What {{good}} shows for the set of terms with a normal form uses the standardization theorem (quoted there). The new lemma of {{good}} offers a way without it: take `Good = WN` of that lemma. `WN` is expansion-closed by its rule *head step*, contains `B`, has *applied to a variable* by that lemma, and its members have normal forms. Then item "normal" quotes no theorem. Worth one sentence, since the page tracks what each result rests on.
5. Quoted (note): "The proof is the move of {{good}}: the sets are expansion-closed sets inside a set `Good`". It is a variant of that move, not an instance: there the sets are closed under undoing safe head steps and `Base` asks the arguments to be in `Good`; here closure is under every head step, and in the first case `B` has arbitrary arguments (needed for `ω` to the left of an arrow). "A variant of the move of {{good}}" is the accurate phrase.
6. Quoted: "With `Good = SN` it does not start, because `SN` is not expansion-closed". Right (`(λx.y) Ω →h y`). Fine as is.
7. The attribution "gives as Coppo, Dezani and Venneri's" and the two characterizations were not checked by this reader against the survey (not available to me in the time); the mathematical content of the two "if and only if" statements is the standard one.

Simpler. The claim "if `t x ∈ HN` then `t ∈ HN`" has a two-line proof by the leftmost head run instead of an arbitrary run, but that would need "head reduction reaches a head normal form if any run does", which is a quoted theorem; the proof given, on an arbitrary run, is the self-contained one. Keep it.

## 4. `implicit.html`: note of the definition "Intersection over the members of a type set"

**Verdict: correct.** For `u ∈ SN` and a type set `W`, `{t : t u ∈ W}` is a type set, and `Π_{u∈T} V(u)` is the intersection of the sets `{t : t u ∈ V(u)}` over `u ∈ T`, by the definition of the dependent arrow in section 25 (`{t : t u ∈ V(u) for every u ∈ T}`). The quotation and the report of the two quantifiers agree with the guide, with one imprecision (finding 4.2).

Re-derivation. SN condition: `t u ∈ W ⊆ SN`, so `t ∈ SN` by R1. Base condition: `x u1..un u` is in `Base` because `u ∈ SN`, and `Base ⊆ W`. Closure condition: `s →h s′` safe and `s′ u ∈ W`; `s u →h s′ u` is safe with the same argument; `W` is closed. In the intersection each `u ∈ T` is in `SN` by the SN condition of `T` and each `V(u)` is a type set, and `T` is not empty.

Findings.

1. Quoted: "by the proof of the arrow lemma read at the one argument `u`". Two of the three conditions do read off that proof at the argument `u`. The SN condition does not: the arrow lemma proves it at a *variable* argument ("A variable `x` is in `T`"), which is not the one argument `u`. The step still holds, with `u` in the place of the variable (`t u ∈ W ⊆ SN`, R1). And the hypothesis `u ∈ SN` is used in the Base condition only. Suggested wording: "is a type set: the proof of the arrow lemma goes through with `u` as the only argument, the SN condition using `u` where that proof uses a variable".
2. Quoted: "Krivine’s realizability has the same pair, for a quantifier over the elements of a set". Definition 10.8 is for any name `a`: `‖∀x^a φ(x)‖ = ⋃_{b ∈ dom(a)} ‖φ(b)‖` (a union of sets of stacks, so the realizers are an intersection: the implicit form). Definition 13.10 is not for any set: it is for the ordinal names `α̂` only, `‖∀x^α̂ φ(x)‖ = ⋃_{β∈α} {ν_β.π : π ∈ ‖φ(β̂)‖}`, where `ν_β` is the `β`-th term of a fixed enumeration of all terms. So the second form exists for sets with a chosen coding of their elements by terms. Fix: "for a quantifier over the elements of a set, and in the second form over the elements of an ordinal".
3. The words “without reference to which witness we are working with” are in the guide at the start of section 13.1, said of the quantifier over reish(a) in the sense of Definition 10.8: exact. "The stack begins with a term that codes the element, so that the proof is told which one it is" reports Definition 13.10 and Remark 13.8 ("our falsity value tells us which element of `α̂` our `x` is meant to correspond to"); Remark 13.8 is about `‖x ɛ̸ α̂‖`, and Definition 13.10 carries it to the quantifier. Fair.
4. A difference the sentence could name in four words: in the dependent arrow the argument is the member `u` itself; in the guide it is a code `ν_β` of the element, a term that need not be related to `β̂` otherwise. The page says "a term that codes the element", which is accurate.

Simpler. The sentence can be shortened to the identity itself: "`Π_{u∈T} V(u) = ⋂_{u∈T} {t : t u ∈ V(u)}`, and each of these sets is a type set; the implicit product is `⋂_{u∈T} V(u)`."
