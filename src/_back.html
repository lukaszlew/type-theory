Status of the claims

Each lemma and theorem carries a one-word status in its header: textbook, quoted from a listed source, here, reviewed for a result worked out for this page and re-derived by an independent reader, or here, unreviewed. This list gives the same information by topic, with what the one word cannot say.

  • Textbook. The definition of a type set, the arrow lemma, the intersection lemma, the theorem and both corollaries. This is the short form of Girard’s proof that uses Tait’s conditions. The definition of a reducibility candidate. The Church–Rosser theorem, the standardization theorem and subject reduction for the kinding rules of Fω, each quoted where it is used. That head steps commute with other steps, in {{families}}, is part of the proof of the standardization theorem; the proof given there is this page’s.

That unions of candidates are candidates, with the quotation on what was believed before, is Riba’s theorem; the two-line proof for this setting in {{cmpsets}} is this page’s. The two self-type rules. What the remark on self types reports about System S and about the Calculus of Dependent Lambda Eliminations, including its two quotations. The rules for the implicit product and for the equality, quoted from the paper on Cedille Core with the terms erased. What Stump’s semantics does, and the two quotations on normalization, from his paper on the syntax and semantics of Cedille. The characterization theorem of {{andsn}}, which secondary sources attribute to Coppo and Dezani for terms that never discard an argument and to Pottinger for all terms; Krivine’s book treats System D, and its theorem numbers were not checked. What {{and}} says about ω, from Kikuchi’s paper, and what {{andlaws}} says about intersection types that count uses, from Bernadet and Graham-Lengrand. The form ιx:A.B and its two rules, which are Kopylov’s. What the second remark of {{tests}}, the last paragraph of the remark in {{hyp}} and the row on tests in {{cmpsem}} say about Krivine’s realizability, from Matthews’s guide; the guide treats set theory, and what it says was not compared with Krivine’s own papers. What {{forget}}, the closing remark of {{erasure}}, {{cube}} and {{allkinds}} say about Geuvers’s proof for the Calculus of Constructions, from his paper of 1994. What {{induct}} says about the upper monotonization and about Mendler’s rules, what {{families}} says about the two arrows, the sentence on candidates in {{cmpsets}} and the name “extended induction” in {{more}}, from Matthes’s thesis; Mendler’s own papers were not read. What {{complete}} and the row on the simple semantics in {{cmpsem}} say about the system of Barendregt, Coppo and Dezani, about Hindley’s theorem and about the three readings of the arrow in models, from van Bakel’s survey and from the paper of Dezani, Honsell and Alessi; the two papers of 1983 themselves were not read. The names orthogonal and biorthogonal, the place of the sets defined by tests among Tait’s and Girard’s, that they are candidates, what the example on unions in {{tests}} reports about existential types, and what {{families}} says about the values of a candidate and about hereditary neutral terms, from Riba’s paper on the values of reducibility candidates. What {{equalities}} says about a cast between type expressions and about strict propositions, and what {{price}} says about Girard’s operator J, from the paper of Abel and Coquand. The example in {{limits}} of a step that loses a type expression, from Riba’s paper, and what limit 4 there reports from Geuvers and from Riba. What the notes and remarks of {{mu}} and {{classical}} report of the λμ-calculus, from the papers of van Bakel, Barbanera and de’Liguoro, of Battyányi and Nour, of Nour and Saber, of David and Nour and of Matthes, and from Rozière’s lecture notes; Parigot’s own papers were not read. What {{tests}} reports of test-defined relations and of the two terms of Riba’s example, and {{pairs}} of identity extension, from the papers of Pitts and of Plotkin and Abadi.
