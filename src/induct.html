The natural numbers have appeared three times: as the Church numerals cNat with induction in {{implicit}}, as the least fixed point of {{more}} whose members carry their predecessor, and as the Nat of {{self}}, which is the Church numerals again under a self type. This section puts the ways of building an inductive type side by side, adds a third in its general form, which is Mendler’s, and says what each costs in this semantics. The general form was chosen because Cedille uses it with good results, and the question was whether those results survive the move from Cedille’s rules to sets.

The Mendler-style fixed point

Definition · Mendler algebra, and the fixed point

Let F be any function from type sets to type sets. For a type set D, the Mendler algebras into D are

Alg(F, D) = ⋂C ((C ⇒ D) ⇒ (F(C) ⇒ D))

with C over all type sets, and the Mendler fixed point of F is

Fix(F) = ⋂D (Alg(F, D) ⇒ D)

The constructor is the term in = λu.λa. a (λr. r a) u.

A member of Alg(F, D) is one layer of a recursion: given a way c to handle a smaller thing of any type set C, it handles a thing of F(C). A member of Fix(F) runs any such layer. The name is Mendler’s, and the form is the one Firsov, Blair and Stump use in Cedille. Nothing is asked of F: not that it is monotone, and not that Fix(F) is a fixed point of it in any sense. The name “fixed point” is kept because it is the usual one.

Lemma · The Mendler fixed point, for any Fhere, reviewed

Let F be any function from type sets to type sets.

type set
Fix(F) is a type set, and so is Alg(F, D) for every type set D.
in
in ∈ F(Fix(F)) ⇒ Fix(F).
fold
If a ∈ Alg(F, D), then λz. z a ∈ Fix(F) ⇒ D, and (λz. z a) (in y) reaches a (λr. r a) y by three head steps: the layer a is run on y, with λr. r a as the way to handle a smaller thing.
Proof

Type set. Both are built from type sets by ⇒, by F, and by intersections indexed by the collection of all type sets. The arrow lemma and the intersection lemma of {{ops}} apply.

in. Take u ∈ F(Fix(F)). It is to be shown that in u ∈ Fix(F): that for every type set D and every a ∈ Alg(F, D), in u a ∈ D. The term in u a reaches a (λr. r a) u by two head steps, both safe, because u and a are in SN. Instantiate a at C = Fix(F): a ∈ (Fix(F) ⇒ D) ⇒ (F(Fix(F)) ⇒ D). And λr. r a ∈ Fix(F) ⇒ D, by abstract: if r ∈ Fix(F), then instantiate r at D and apply to a, so r a ∈ D. So a (λr. r a) ∈ F(Fix(F)) ⇒ D, and a (λr. r a) u ∈ D. The closure condition, twice, gives in u a ∈ D.

Fold. If z ∈ Fix(F), then z a ∈ D by instantiate and apply, so λz. z a ∈ Fix(F) ⇒ D by abstract. The head steps are (λz. z a) (in y) →h in y a, then in y a →h (λa. a (λr. r a) y) a, then one step to a (λr. r a) y. ∎

The inductive part, for monotone F

Induction says more than fold: the result may mention the member itself, as in {{self}} and {{implicit}}. For that the recursion layer must be allowed to assume the induction hypothesis for the smaller things, and “smaller” now has to mean “inside the inductive type”, not “of any type set”. The index set of the intersection shrinks accordingly.

Definition · Proof algebras, and the inductive part

Let F be a monotone function from type sets to type sets, in the sense of {{more}}. For a type set C and a family Q, the proof algebras are

PrfAlg(C, Q) = ⋂D ⊆ C ( Πr∈D Q(r) ⇒ Πy∈F(D) Q(in y) )

an intersection indexed by the type sets D inside C, in the sense of {{ops}}; the index set contains Min, so it is not empty. Let

G(C) = { t : t ∈ Fix(F) and t ∈ ⋂Q (PrfAlg(C, Q) ⇒ Q(t)) }

with Q over all families, and let the inductive part Ind(F) be the least fixed point μG of {{more}}.

A member of PrfAlg(C, Q) turns a proof of Q for every member of a type set D inside C into a proof of Q(in y) for every y ∈ F(D). G(C) is a dependent intersection of {{dinter}}, whose second part is a family because each Q is.

Lemma · The inductive part, for monotone Fhere, reviewed

Let F be monotone.

type set
G is monotone, so Ind(F) = μG exists, is a type set, and equals G(Ind(F)). It is inside Fix(F).
in
in ∈ F(Ind(F)) ⇒ Ind(F).
induction
For every family Q and every p ∈ PrfAlg(Ind(F), Q): if e ∈ Ind(F), then e p ∈ Q(e). So λe. e p ∈ Πe∈Ind(F) Q(e).
out
Let out = λe. e (λf.λy.y). Then out ∈ Ind(F) ⇒ F(Ind(F)), and out (in u) reaches u by five head steps, however large u is.
Proof

Type set. If C ⊆ C′, then every D inside C is inside C′, so PrfAlg(C′, Q) ⊆ PrfAlg(C, Q), so PrfAlg(C, Q) ⇒ Q(t) ⊆ PrfAlg(C′, Q) ⇒ Q(t), because a smaller input set gives a larger arrow, and so G(C) ⊆ G(C′). Each G(C) is a type set by the lemmas of {{ops}}, {{darrow}} and {{dinter}}. So μG exists and G(μG) = μG, by {{more}}. It is inside Fix(F) by the definition of G.

Write V(t) for ⋂Q (PrfAlg(Ind(F), Q) ⇒ Q(t)), so that Ind(F) = { t : t ∈ Fix(F) and t ∈ V(t) }.

in. Take u ∈ F(Ind(F)). Since Ind(F) ⊆ Fix(F) and F is monotone, u ∈ F(Fix(F)), and in u ∈ Fix(F) by the lemma on the Mendler fixed point. For in u ∈ V(in u), take any family Q and p ∈ PrfAlg(Ind(F), Q). As before, in u p reaches p (λr. r p) u by two safe head steps. Instantiate p at D = Ind(F), which is inside itself: p ∈ Πr∈Ind(F) Q(r) ⇒ Πy∈F(Ind(F)) Q(in y). And λr. r p ∈ Πr∈Ind(F) Q(r), by abstract: if r ∈ Ind(F), then r ∈ V(r), so instantiate at Q and apply to p give r p ∈ Q(r). So p (λr. r p) u ∈ Q(in u), and the closure condition, twice, gives in u p ∈ Q(in u).

Induction. If e ∈ Ind(F), then e ∈ V(e), so e p ∈ Q(e) by instantiate and apply. Then abstract for the dependent arrow.

Out. Let Q be the constant family with value F(Ind(F)). Then λf.λy.y ∈ PrfAlg(Ind(F), Q): for any D inside Ind(F), any f ∈ Πr∈D F(Ind(F)) and any y ∈ F(D), the term (λf.λy.y) f y reaches y by two head steps, safe because f and y are in SN, and y ∈ F(D) ⊆ F(Ind(F)) because F is monotone. So by induction, e (λf.λy.y) ∈ F(Ind(F)) for every e ∈ Ind(F), and abstract gives out ∈ Ind(F) ⇒ F(Ind(F)). The head steps: out (in u) →h in u (λf.λy.y), then two steps to (λf.λy.y) (λr. r (λf.λy.y)) u, then two steps to u. ∎

Definition · The upper monotonization of F

Let F be any function from type sets to type sets. For a type set C, the upper monotonization of F at C is

F⊇(C) = ⋃D ⊆ C F(D)

the union of the type sets F(D) over the type sets D inside C.

The name is Matthes’s. Quoted from his thesis: the rules of Mendler’s system, in the variant that the thesis studies, are the rules of the least fixed point of F⊇, and not of F. That is why they need nothing of F: Mendler restricted his system to positive F, and Matthes proves strong normalization without the restriction.

Lemma · The inductive part, for any Fhere, reviewed

The definitions of PrfAlg, G and Ind(F) do not use that F is monotone. Read them for any function F from type sets to type sets. Then:

monotonization
F⊇(C) is a type set, F⊇ is monotone, F(C) ⊆ F⊇(C), and F⊇ = F if and only if F is monotone.
type set, induction
The items type set and induction of the lemma on the inductive part for monotone F hold as stated.
in
in is in F⊇(Ind(F)) ⇒ Ind(F), and so in F(Ind(F)) ⇒ Ind(F).
out
out is in Ind(F) ⇒ F⊇(Ind(F)): what out returns is in F(D) for some type set D inside Ind(F).

So in and out pass between Ind(F) and F⊇(Ind(F)) for every F, and the hypothesis that F is monotone does one thing only: it puts F in the place of F⊇ on both sides. The constructor and induction are free, as the constructor and fold of Fix(F) are. The form of in with F⊇ was pointed out by the reader of the sixth review.

Proof

Monotonization. The collection of the type sets inside C is not empty, because C is inside itself, so the union is a type set, by the union lemma of {{ops}}, and it contains F(C). A larger C has more type sets inside it, so F⊇ is monotone. If F is monotone, every F(D) with D ⊆ C is inside F(C), so the union is F(C); and if F⊇ = F, then F is monotone because F⊇ is.

Type set and induction. The proofs given for monotone F do not use the hypothesis: that G is monotone follows from the index set D ⊆ C alone, and induction is the definition of G read off.

in. Take u ∈ F(D′) for a type set D′ inside Ind(F). First, in u ∈ Fix(F). Take any type set D and any a ∈ Alg(F, D). The term in u a reaches a (λr. r a) u by two safe head steps, because u and a are in SN. Instantiate a at C = D′, and not at Fix(F) as the proof of the lemma on the Mendler fixed point did: a ∈ (D′ ⇒ D) ⇒ (F(D′) ⇒ D). And λr. r a ∈ D′ ⇒ D, by abstract: if r ∈ D′, then r ∈ Ind(F), so r ∈ Fix(F), and instantiate at D and apply give r a ∈ D. So a (λr. r a) u ∈ D, and the closure condition, twice, gives in u a ∈ D. Second, in u ∈ V(in u), where V(t) is ⋂Q (PrfAlg(Ind(F), Q) ⇒ Q(t)) as in the proof for monotone F. Take any family Q and p ∈ PrfAlg(Ind(F), Q), and instantiate p at the index D′, which is inside Ind(F): p ∈ Πr∈D′ Q(r) ⇒ Πy∈F(D′) Q(in y). And λr. r p ∈ Πr∈D′ Q(r), because r ∈ D′ gives r ∈ Ind(F), so r ∈ V(r) and r p ∈ Q(r). So p (λr. r p) u ∈ Q(in u); the term in u p reaches it by two safe head steps, and the closure condition gives in u p ∈ Q(in u).

Out. Let Q be the constant family with value F⊇(Ind(F)). Then λf.λy.y ∈ PrfAlg(Ind(F), Q): for any type set D inside Ind(F), any f in Πr∈D Q(r) and any y ∈ F(D), the term (λf.λy.y) f y reaches y by two safe head steps, and y ∈ F(D) ⊆ F⊇(Ind(F)), by the definition of the upper monotonization. So by induction, e (λf.λy.y) ∈ F⊇(Ind(F)) for every e ∈ Ind(F), and abstract gives the claim. ∎

Example · Mendler-style natural numbers

Let F(C) = ⋂D (D ⇒ ((C ⇒ D) ⇒ D)), the function of the example “Least fixed points” in {{more}}: F(C) contains the zero λz.λs.z and, for each n ∈ C, the successor λz.λs.s n, which carries its predecessor. It is monotone. Then

0 = in (λz.λs.z)   S = λn. in (λz.λs.s n)   pred = λe. out e 0 (λm.m)

By the lemma on the Mendler fixed point and the lemma on the inductive part for monotone F, 0 ∈ Ind(F), S ∈ Ind(F) ⇒ Ind(F), and pred ∈ Ind(F) ⇒ Ind(F): out e is in F(Ind(F)), instantiate it at D = Ind(F), and apply to 0 and to λm.m ∈ Ind(F) ⇒ Ind(F). And pred (S n) reaches n by ten head steps, whatever n is: one for pred, one for out, one that takes S n to in (λz.λs.s n), four for in and λf.λy.y, two that apply λz.λs.s n to 0 and to λm.m, and one for (λm.m) n. Induction holds with PrfAlg(Ind(F), Q) as the induction step: a member must give Q(in y) for every y ∈ F(D), among them the zero and the successors of members r ∈ D, from a proof of Q(r) for every r ∈ D.

The Church numerals of {{implicit}} have no such predecessor: there, as Firsov, Blair and Stump report, citing Parigot, computing the predecessor takes a number of steps that grows with the number. That is the reason Cedille uses this encoding. The claims about the Church numerals are quoted and not proved here.

Remark · What Cedille’s casts do, and what replaces them here

Quoted. In Cedille the inductive part is built without a fixed point. Its proof algebras quantify over every type R together with a cast from R to the fixed point, a function that is provably equal to the identity; so its inductive part is, in the terms of this section, G(Fix(F)). Its induction principle for proof algebras over the inductive part itself is then derived by using the induction hypothesis for the members of R to build a cast from R into the inductive part. A cast is applied by introducing a dependent intersection [x, y {p}] whose two parts are joined by a proof p that the cast is the identity, and projecting; Cedille Core, from which {{equality}} takes its rules, replaced that annotation by the rule φ, which does the same retyping. Firsov, Blair and Stump also replace functoriality of F by an identity mapping, which lifts casts to casts.

Worked out for this page. Here a cast between type sets is an inclusion D ⊆ C, and it appears as a condition on the index set of the intersection, not as a hypothesis, because {{equality}} showed that φ is false in this semantics and limit 1 of {{limits}} says why a hypothesis cannot carry it. An identity mapping becomes the condition that F is monotone, which is needed only to put F in the place of F⊇, by the lemma on the inductive part for any F. G(Fix(F)), Cedille’s inductive part, has in and induction for proof algebras over Fix(F) by the same proof, with no fixed point. What it lacks is the passage to proof algebras over the inductive part itself, which is the step Cedille makes by building a cast from the induction hypothesis; and it is that passage that out needs, since λf.λy.y is a proof algebra over Ind(F) and not over Fix(F). The least fixed point is what replaces the cast. Ind(F) is inside G(Fix(F)), because G is monotone and Ind(F) = G(Ind(F)) with Ind(F) ⊆ Fix(F); whether the two are the same type set was not settled for this page. The obstacle is the one of {{hyp}}: the induction principle of G(Fix(F)) quantifies over proof algebras p, and a p with a free variable at its head passes every requirement, so nothing about a member of G(Fix(F)) can be read off from the principle alone. The Mendler fixed point Fix(F) itself, its constructor and fold cost nothing and ask nothing of F, which is one of the points Firsov, Blair and Stump make, and it survives the move from rules to sets.
