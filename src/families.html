{{cmpsets}} put Girard’s candidates beside the type sets of {{typeset}}. There is a third collection between the two, and the three stand in a simple relation: they are one structure read three ways, as terms with their evaluation history, as sets of values, and as sets of values closed under running. The main line never needs this section. It says what the choice made in {{typeset}} means, and it makes exact the picture given there, that a type set is roughly a set of values.

Values, and the third collection

Definition · Value, evaluation, head-closed type set

A value is a strongly normalizing abstraction.

Let t be a strongly normalizing term. Its head steps are steps, so they end, at a term with no head step: an abstraction, or a term x u1…un. That term is the evaluation of t, written eval(t). It is a value, or it is in Base; in the second case t is said to get stuck.

A type set T is head-closed if the following holds: if s ∈ T and s →h s′, then s′ ∈ T.

Every head step of a strongly normalizing term is safe, because its argument is a subterm. So a head-closed type set is one that is closed under safe head steps in both directions. The lemma “Head-closed type sets are sets of values” shows that its members are exactly its values together with every term that evaluates to one of them, together with Min. The name “head-closed” is this page’s. From general knowledge, and not checked against sources: sets of this shape, every term that evaluates into a chosen set of values, are what the semantics of programming languages builds from a set of values, as in Harper’s note, and what proofs by tests produce for closed terms; the head-closed type sets are their form for open terms. One source was read for the closed terms: in Allen’s thesis, which {{pairs}} quotes, membership in the relation of a type expression is defined through the value that a closed term evaluates to. We found no name for the collection in the literature, and that is the price of working with it. The half of it that concerns candidates is in the literature: Riba states, with a reference to another paper of his for the proof, that a reducibility candidate is determined by its values, where a value is a term that an elimination, here an application, can act on, which for β is an abstraction; that is the item candidates of the lemma “Head-closed type sets are sets of values”. He calls the members of the smallest candidate, the Min of this page, the “hereditary neutral terms”: the strongly normalizing terms that are not values and never reduce to one.

The chain

Write SAT for the collection of all type sets, HC for the collection of head-closed type sets, and CR for the collection of reducibility candidates. The name HC is for this section; the letters SAT and CR are the usual ones for Tait’s and Girard’s collections.

Lemma · Head steps commute with other stepstextbook

Let t →h r be a safe head step, and let t → t′ be any step. Then t′ = r, or t′ →h r′ by a safe head step with r reaching r′ by zero or more steps.

So if t reaches r by k safe head steps and t reaches t′ by any steps, then t′ reaches, by at most k safe head steps, a term that r reaches by steps.

This is the textbook fact that head steps commute with the other steps, which is the heart of the standardization theorem in Barendregt’s book. The proof is given because the page needs the exact form with safe steps.

Proof

Let t = (λx.u) v v1…vn and r = u[v/x] v1…vn. The step t → t′ is the head step itself, and then t′ = r; or it is inside u, inside v, or inside some vi.

Inside u, with u → u′: t′ = (λx.u′) v v⃗ →h u′[v/x] v⃗, and u[v/x] v⃗ → u′[v/x] v⃗ by one step. Inside v, with v → v′: t′ →h u[v′/x] v⃗, and u[v/x] v⃗ reaches it by one step for each free occurrence of x in u. Inside vi: likewise. In each case the new head step is safe, because its argument is v or a reduct of v, and reducts of strongly normalizing terms are strongly normalizing, by R1 (subterms).

The second statement is proved by induction on k. If k = 0, then r = t, and t′ itself is the term wanted. Otherwise t →h t1 and t1 reaches r by k − 1 safe head steps. First, t′ is a reduct of t1, or t′ →h t1′ by a safe head step for some reduct t1′ of t1. This is shown by induction on the number of steps from t to t′: along the way, either the head step has been taken, after which every term is a reduct of t1, or the term at hand still has a safe head step to a reduct of t1, and the first statement carries that through the next step. Then the induction hypothesis, applied to the k − 1 head steps from t1 to r and to the reduct of t1 at hand, gives a term that r reaches by steps and that the reduct reaches by at most k − 1 safe head steps; with the one head step from t′, if there was one, that is at most k. ∎

Lemma · The three collections form a chainhere, reviewed

chain
CR ⊆ HC ⊆ SAT.
same operations
Each collection is closed under the intersection and under the union of a nonempty set of its members, computed as sets. So every nonempty set of members has a largest member below it and a smallest member above it, which is what “complete lattice” means in standard terms; and these are computed in the same way in all three.
same ends
Min and SN belong to all three collections. Min is the smallest member and SN the largest, in each.
Proof

Chain. A candidate is closed under all steps, by the lemma in {{cmpsets}}, so under head steps.

Same operations. For SAT this is the intersection lemma and the union lemma of {{ops}}. Closure under head steps and closure under all steps are conditions on single members, so they pass to intersections and unions.

Same ends. SN is closed under every step, by R1 (subterms). For Min: let s ∈ Min, so s reaches a member b of Base by safe head steps, and let s → s′. By the lemma on commuting steps, s′ reaches by safe head steps a term b′ that b reaches by steps. A reduct of x u1…un is x u′1…u′n, which is in Base. So s′ ∈ Min. ∎

Going down and coming back

Definition · Four conversions between the collections

For a type set T:

hc(T) = cl(Th), where Th is T together with every term that a member of T reaches by head steps.

cr(T) = cl(T→), where T→ is T together with every term that a member of T reaches by steps.

kh(T) is the set of the members t of T such that every term that t reaches by head steps is in T.

k(T) is the set of the members t of T such that every term that t reaches by steps is in T.

cl is the closure of {{typeset}}: add every term that reaches a member by safe head steps. The first two conversions enlarge a type set, the last two shrink it.

Lemma · Each conversion is the nearest memberhere, reviewed

smallest above
hc(T) is the smallest head-closed type set that contains T, and cr(T) is the smallest candidate that contains T.
largest below
kh(T) is the largest head-closed type set inside T, and k(T) is the largest candidate inside T.
nothing lost going up
If T is head-closed, then hc(T) = kh(T) = T; if T is a candidate, then cr(T) = k(T) = T.

So a conversion from a smaller collection to a larger one is the identity, and the two conversions back are a closure and a kernel: the nearest member of the smaller collection above, and the nearest below.

Proof

cr(T) is a candidate containing T. It contains T, so it contains Base, and it is inside SN by R1 (subterms) and R3 (safe head step). It is closed under safe head expansion, being a closure. For closure under steps: let t ∈ cl(T→), so t reaches some r ∈ T→ by safe head steps, and let t → t′. By the lemma on commuting steps, t′ reaches by safe head steps a term r′ that r reaches by steps, and r′ ∈ T→, because T→ is closed under steps. So t′ ∈ cl(T→).

cr(T) is the smallest. A candidate that contains T contains T→, by closure under steps, and then cl(T→), by the closure condition.

k(T) is a candidate inside T. Base ⊆ k(T), because a reduct of a member of Base is in Base ⊆ T. k(T) is closed under steps, because a reduct of a reduct is a reduct. For the closure condition: let s →h s′ be safe with s′ ∈ k(T), and let s″ be any term that s reaches by steps. By the lemma on commuting steps, s″ reaches, by at most one safe head step, a term that s′ reaches by steps; that term is in T, because s′ ∈ k(T); so s″ ∈ T, by the closure condition of T or because s″ is that term. Hence s ∈ k(T).

k(T) is the largest. A candidate inside T has all reducts of its members inside itself, so inside T.

Nothing lost going up is immediate from the definitions. The statements for hc and kh have the same proofs, with head steps in the place of steps; the commuting lemma is not even needed, because a head step from t stays on the head reduction of t. ∎

Example · What the conversions do to Gen({I I})

hc(Gen({I I})) = cr(Gen({I I})) = Gen({I}): going up adds I, and I I was already in Gen({I}). Coming back the other way, kh(Gen({I I})) = k(Gen({I I})) = Min: the only members of Gen({I I}) whose head reducts stay inside are the members of Min. The conversions lose the one thing Gen({I I}) records: that I I is in and its reduct is not.

The three readings

Lemma · Head-closed type sets are sets of valueshere, reviewed

Send a head-closed type set T to the set of values it contains, and send a set W of values to

TW = { t ∈ SN : eval(t) ∈ W, or t gets stuck }

bijection
The two maps are inverse to each other, between HC and the sets of values.
operations
Intersection and union of head-closed type sets are intersection and union of their sets of values. For a value v, Gen({v}) is head-closed and its set of values is {v}.
candidates
A head-closed type set is a candidate if and only if its set of values is closed under steps.
tell apart
Two strongly normalizing terms belong to the same head-closed type sets if and only if they have the same evaluation, or both get stuck. Compare the lemma in {{structure}}: all type sets together tell apart every two terms outside Min.
not so for SAT
A type set is not determined by its values: Gen({I I}) and Min contain no value at all, and differ.

So HC is, up to the bijection, the collection of all sets of values: a type set in HC is just a choice of which values belong. CR is the sets of values that are closed under steps. And SAT keeps, besides the values, the history: which terms on the way to a value are in and which are out.

Proof

TW is a head-closed type set with values W. It is inside SN by definition, and it contains Base, whose members are stuck. If s →h s′ is safe and s′ ∈ TW, then s ∈ SN by R3 (safe head step), and eval(s) = eval(s′), so s ∈ TW. If s ∈ TW and s →h s′, then eval(s′) = eval(s), so s′ ∈ TW. A value has no head step, so the values in TW are the values v with eval(v) = v ∈ W, which is W.

A head-closed T is TW for its set of values W. Let t ∈ T. Then eval(t) ∈ T, because T is closed under head steps. If eval(t) is a value, it is in W; otherwise t gets stuck. So T ⊆ TW. Conversely, let t ∈ TW. If eval(t) ∈ W ⊆ T, then t ∈ T by the closure condition, applied along the head steps from t to eval(t), which are safe. If t gets stuck, then t ∈ Min ⊆ T.

Operations. The values of an intersection or a union are the intersection or the union of the values. Gen({v}) = cl(Base ∪ {v}) is T{v}: a term is in it if and only if it reaches v or a member of Base by safe head steps.

Candidates. If T is a candidate, then a step inside a value of T gives a value of T, by closure under steps. Conversely, let W be closed under steps, let t ∈ TW and t → t′. If t gets stuck, then t ∈ Min, and t′ ∈ Min by the lemma on the chain. Otherwise t reaches eval(t) ∈ W by safe head steps, and by the lemma on commuting steps t′ reaches by safe head steps a term w′ that eval(t) reaches by steps. A reduct of an abstraction is an abstraction, so w′ is a value, and w′ ∈ W. So eval(t′) = w′ ∈ W, and t′ ∈ TW.

Tell apart. The head-closed type sets that contain a term t are the TW with eval(t) ∈ W, or all of them if t gets stuck. So two terms are in the same ones if and only if their evaluations are the same value, or both get stuck.

Not so for SAT. Min contains no abstraction, because every member reaches a member of Base by head steps and an abstraction has none. Gen({I I}) adds the terms that reach I I by safe head steps, none of which is an abstraction. ∎

Definition · The arrow generated by abstractions

For type sets T and U, T ⇒I U is the type set generated, in the sense of {{typeset}}, by the abstractions λx.t with t[u/x] ∈ U for every u ∈ T.

Those abstractions are in SN: the variable x is in T, so t ∈ U. So the definition is of the form Gen(S) for a set S of strongly normalizing terms.

Lemma · The arrow into a head-closed type set, in valueshere, reviewed

Let T be a type set and let U be a head-closed type set.

head-closed
T ⇒ U is head-closed.
values
The values in T ⇒ U are exactly the abstractions λx.t with t[u/x] ∈ U for every u ∈ T.
generated
So T ⇒ U = T ⇒I U: a member is a term that evaluates to an abstraction whose body sends T into U, or a term that gets stuck.
two arrows
For type sets T and U in general, T ⇒I U is inside T ⇒ U, and the facts apply and abstract of {{facts}} hold for it as they do for T ⇒ U. It can be strictly smaller: for T = SN and U = Gen({ I u : u ∈ SN }), the term I is in T ⇒ U and not in T ⇒I U. The inclusion and the example are Matthes’s, read here for untyped terms.

So there are two arrows. T ⇒ U takes every term that sends T into U; T ⇒I U takes the abstractions whose bodies do, with Base and what the closure condition adds. Quoted from Matthes’s thesis: he calls them the elimination-based and the introduction-based arrow, proves the second inside the first, gives an example where they differ, and observes that the proof of strong normalization uses only what both have, which here is the two facts of {{facts}}. This page uses the first, and Harper’s note, in {{harper}}, defines the second and leaves the first as an exercise. Matthes adds that the second cannot be used if a constructor is a constant with an arrow for its type expression and not a way of forming terms, and that the first has no such limit. A footnote of his names a paper of McAllester, Kucan and Otth in which, in his words, “instead of checking whether a term is in the predicate one checks whether its weak head normal form is in the predicate”; from the footnote alone that looks like the head-closed collection, and the paper was not read for this page. The lemma says where the choice goes away: into a head-closed type set there is one arrow, and it is a head-closed type set, which the lemma “Head-closed type sets are sets of values” identifies with a set of values. The example in the item two arrows is of the same kind as every other difference between the two collections: U contains I u and not always the term u that its head step leads to.

Proof

Head-closed. Let s ∈ T ⇒ U and s →h s′. For every u ∈ T, the term s u is in U and s u →h s′ u, because a head step survives one more argument. So s′ u ∈ U, because U is head-closed.

Values. An abstraction λx.t is in T ⇒ U if and only if (λx.t) u ∈ U for every u ∈ T. The term (λx.t) u takes a head step to t[u/x], and the step is safe, because u ∈ SN. If t[u/x] ∈ U, then (λx.t) u ∈ U by the closure condition. If (λx.t) u ∈ U, then t[u/x] ∈ U, because U is head-closed.

Generated. By the lemma “Head-closed type sets are sets of values”, a head-closed type set is TW for its set W of values, and TW is Gen(W), by M2 (generated): its members are the strongly normalizing terms whose head steps lead to a member of W or of Base.

Two arrows. Write W for the set of the abstractions λx.t with t[u/x] ∈ U for every u ∈ T. Each member of W is in T ⇒ U, by abstract; and T ⇒ U is a type set, so it contains Gen(W), the smallest type set that contains W. So apply holds for T ⇒I U, and abstract holds by its definition.

For the example, I u ∈ SN for u ∈ SN, by R3 (safe head step), so U is a type set, and I ∈ SN ⇒ U. The term K is not in U: it has no head step, it is not in Base, and it is not of the form I u. So x[K/x] ∉ U, and I, which is λx.x, is not in W. And I has no head step and is not in Base, so it is not in Gen(W), by M2 (generated). ∎

Lemma · Only the head-closed type sets have complementshere, reviewed

In HC every type set T has a complement: T′ = (SN ∖ T) ∪ Min is head-closed, T ∩ T′ = Min and T ∪ T′ = SN. In SAT and in CR some type sets have no such complement: Gen({I I}) in SAT, and TI in CR.

So in HC a choice of values can be negated, and in the other two collections it cannot. In standard terms, HC is a power set up to Min, and so a complete Boolean algebra, and the other two are not. No result on this page uses this; it is listed because it is the sharpest way to say what the head-closed type sets are.

What a complement is not. The negation of System F is A → ∀X.X, which names ⟦A⟧ ⇒ Min: a type set in every collection, and not the complement of ⟦A⟧. The two differ as intuitionistic and classical negation differ, as the example “The arrow into Min is negation” in {{ops}} shows: T ∩ (T ⇒ Min) has no closed member but is not empty, and T ∪ (T ⇒ Min) need not be SN. So the page never needs a complement, and working in the largest collection costs nothing here. Nor would a complement give a rule even in HC: a member of the complement of ⟦A⟧ is a term that evaluates to a value outside ⟦A⟧, and no term witnesses that of another, so no typing rule could conclude it; it is one more set that no type expression names, as most are by {{allsets}}. Classical reasoning enters the semantics by another door, the tests of {{tests}}, where a continuation is the witness.

Proof

T′ is TW′ for the set W′ of values not in T: a strongly normalizing term is in T′ if and only if it gets stuck or evaluates to a value outside T. So T′ is head-closed, and the two equations hold by the items bijection and operations. In SAT, a type set T′ with Gen({I I}) ∪ T′ = SN contains I, which is not in Gen({I I}), and then I I by the closure condition; but I I is in Gen({I I}) and not in Min, so the intersection is not Min. In CR, a candidate T′ with TI ∪ T′ = SN contains λx.I x, which is not in TI, and then I by closure under steps; but I ∈ TI and I ∉ Min. ∎

Example · Three type sets, one from each collection

  • Gen({I I}) is a type set and not head-closed: it contains I I, and I I →h I, and I ∉ Gen({I I}).
  • Gen({λx.I x}) is head-closed, by the item operations: its only value is λx.I x. It is not a candidate: λx.I x → I by a step inside the abstraction, and I ∉ Gen({λx.I x}).
  • TI = Gen({I}) is a candidate, by the item candidates: its only value is I, which has no step.

Lemma · Each collection carries the theoremhere, reviewed

preservation
If T and U are head-closed, then so is T ⇒ U; if they are candidates, then so is T ⇒ U. The intersection of a nonempty set of head-closed type sets, or of candidates, is one again.
theorem
Let ∀X.A name the intersection over all members of HC, or over all members of CR, instead of over all type sets, and let assignments take their values in that collection. Then Lemma N, the four facts, the soundness theorem and both corollaries hold.

Each step down the chain gives up some type sets, and with them some values of X, in return for an invariance of membership: under head steps, then under all steps. The set that a type expression names may change with the collection, because ∀ ranges over fewer sets; for ∀X.X it does not, and for other type expressions this page does not know, as {{cmpsets}} says. The theorem does not change.

Proof

Preservation. Let t ∈ T ⇒ U and t →h t′. For every u ∈ T, t u →h t′ u, because a head step survives one more argument, and t u ∈ U, so t′ u ∈ U by closure of U under head steps. So t′ ∈ T ⇒ U. For a step t → t′ that is not a head step, t u → t′ u is a step, and closure of U under steps gives the same conclusion. Intersections were treated in the lemma on the chain.

Theorem. By the remark in {{rules}} on what the proof asked of the collection that ∀ ranges over, it is enough that the collection consists of type sets, is closed under ⇒ and under nonempty intersections, and has a smallest member with no closed member. The first holds by definition, the second and third are preservation, and the fourth is the item same ends of the lemma on the chain, since Min has no closed member by M3 (smallest). ∎

Remark · What the choice in {{typeset}} means

The definition in {{typeset}} picks the largest of the three collections, the one that asks least of a set. The other two are what remains when a further invariance is asked: that a term and its evaluation share their type sets, or that a term and all its reducts do. Limit 3 of {{limits}}, that a member can leave a type set by running, is the price of the largest collection; the head-closed type sets remove it for head steps and keep it for steps inside abstractions, as Gen({λx.I x}) shows, and the candidates remove it altogether.

The case for each is short. For SAT: it is the largest, every closure proof is one line, and it is the one with a name and a literature. For HC: it is exactly the sets of values, with complements, which is the object that the semantics of programming languages works with; its price is that it has no name in the literature. For CR: a term keeps its type sets while it runs, which is what typing usually promises, and it is Girard’s. The theorem does not care, by the lemma “Each collection carries the theorem”, and nothing in the main line depends on the choice; the main line asks only what the remark in {{rules}} lists. {{pairs}} runs the same theory on pairs of terms, so that a type expression names a relation; for head-closed type sets that is a relation between values. {{tests}} adds a fourth collection below the candidates, the sets defined by tests. A side-by-side reading of every definition and lemma that would change if the page took the head-closed collection as its definition is at sat-or-hc.html.
