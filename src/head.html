One sort of step gets a name of its own, because the definition of a type set in {{typeset}} is stated with it.

Definition · Head step

Every term has exactly one of three forms, with n ≥ 0:

λx.t
an abstraction, for example λx.x x;
x u1…un
a variable applied to arguments, for example y and y δ δ;
(λx.t) u u1…un
an abstraction applied to arguments, for example I I and K K y.

The head step of an abstraction applied to arguments reduces the redex at its front:

(λx.t) u u1…un →h t[u/x] u1…un

The argument of this head step is the term u.

A head step is safe if its argument is in SN.

An abstraction has no head step, and a variable applied to arguments has no head step.

Repeating head steps is called weak head reduction.

A head step survives one more argument: if s →h s′, then s v →h s′ v, with the same argument. {{ops}} uses this.

Example · Head steps

  • K K y →h (λb.K) y →h K. Both head steps are safe: their arguments are K and y.
  • (λx.y) Ω →h y. This head step is not safe, because its argument is Ω.
  • λx.K x has a step, to λx.λb.x, and no head step. y (I I) also has a step and no head step.

Lemma R · Three facts about stepstextbook

R1
Subterms. If t ∈ SN, then every subterm of t is in SN, and every term that t steps to is in SN.
R2
Variable applied. x u1…un ∈ SN if and only if u1,…,un ∈ SN.
R3
Safe head step. If s →h s′ is safe and s′ ∈ SN, then s ∈ SN. Written out: if t[u/x] u1…un ∈ SN and u ∈ SN, then (λx.t) u u1…un ∈ SN.

R3 (safe head step) says that strong normalization survives undoing a safe head step. The word “safe” matters: if x is not free in t, the head step throws u away, and then nothing is known about u.

These three facts are all that the main proof uses about strong normalization.

Proof

R1. A step inside a subterm of t is a step of t. A run from a term that t steps to is the tail of a run from t.

R2. No step can change the variable that is applied, so the term stays a variable applied to arguments. Every step happens inside one of the arguments.

R3. Write s for the term before the head step and s′ for the term after it. The subterms t, u, u1,…,un are all in SN: u by assumption, each ui as a subterm of s′, and t because every step of t is also a step of t[u/x], which is a subterm of s′.

Take any sequence of steps from s. As long as the head step is not taken, each step is inside one of these subterms, and those stop. If the head step is taken, the result is a term that s′ can reach by steps, and s′ ∈ SN. Either way the sequence is finite. ∎

Example · R2 at work

y δ δ ∈ SN by R2 (variable applied), because each argument is in SN. Nothing ever applies the first δ to the second: the variable y stands in the way.

Example · R3 at work, and why it asks for a safe head step

(λx.y) (I I) ∈ SN by R3 (safe head step). Its head step leads to y, which is in SN, and the head step is safe because its argument I I is in SN.

(λx.y) Ω also has a head step to y. That head step is not safe, because its argument Ω is not in SN. And the whole term is not in SN: Ω can be run inside it forever.
